# Story structure for narrated short videos

Use this before drafting narration, together with the topic's own skill (e.g. `historical-storytelling`, `thai-spoiler-shorts`) and `thai-script-review` for Thai wording. It sets the story's shape; the topic skill sets the facts, format and production.

## 1. Decide the question and the answer before writing

Write two lines first:
- **Question:** what the viewer wants to know, in one sentence ("Will the second ship arrive in time?").
- **Answer:** what this clip will deliver, as the evidence supports it ("Yes, barely, and at a price").

If either line is unclear or the evidence cannot support the answer, choose another angle before writing.

## 2. Beats

| Beat | Job | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hook** | Put the conflict, the stake and the difficulty in the opening sentence | Who acts, what is at risk, what must happen, why it is hard. The question must stay open until the reveal |
| **Context** | Give only what the viewer needs to follow the question | Each sentence is the cause of the next. Cut names, dates and side stories that do not change the answer |
| **But (two layers)** | Change the situation | First an obstacle or reversal (they change their minds; a new vote). Then an irony or surprise that comes from the source itself (the defender does not plead for mercy; he argues from self-interest). End this beat on suspense (the vote is almost tied) |
| **Build** | Move toward the answer with concrete, sourced detail | Choose details that visibly close the gap (eating while rowing, sleeping in shifts, no headwind, the other ship slowing). Concrete actions beat adjectives |
| **Reveal** | Answer the hook | Hold it to the last possible moment the evidence allows ("he had just read the order and was about to act, when the second ship came in") |
| **Honest twist** | Show the price and the uncertainty | Say what was not saved or what is disputed ("but not everyone survived"; "scholars question this figure"). Keeps the clip truthful and credible |
| **Close** | Answer the hook again, as a short summary | Ideally three parallel items that sum up why it turned out this way |

Adapt the beats to the material: not every story has a race or a twist, but every script needs a clear question, movement toward its answer, and a payoff in this clip.

## 3. Sentence rules

- Every sentence must raise the stake, add an obstacle, or move toward the answer. Cut any sentence that does none of these.
- Use sourced, concrete details instead of intensifiers ("ate barley mixed with oil and wine as they rowed", not "rowed with all their might").
- Attribute opinions: "Thucydides calls him the most violent man in Athens", not the narrator's verdict.
- Separate fact, single-source claim, interpretation and dispute in the wording itself, not only in a source list.
- Spoken language, short sentences, clear subject in every sentence. For Thai, run `thai-script-review` before generating voice.
- Humour only when it comes from the situation itself (an irony in the record), never invented or at victims' expense.

## 4. Check before voicing

- Can someone state the question after hearing only the opening sentence?
- Does each beat move the question forward, and is the answer delivered in this clip?
- Is every claim in the build and reveal traceable to a source?
- Does the close restate the answer rather than introduce something new?

## Worked example

Mytilene, 427 BC (based on Thucydides, History of the Peloponnesian War, Book 3):
- Hook 0–11 s: the first ship carries an order to kill every man; the next day Athens regrets it; a second ship must catch up a day and a night behind.
- Context 11–36 s: war with Sparta → Mytilene revolts → Sparta sends ships → surrender → angry vote.
- But 36–68 s: second thoughts and a new vote; Diodotus does not ask for mercy but argues the killing would cost Athens; the vote is almost tied.
- Build 68–92 s: wine and barley loaded, rowers eat while rowing and sleep in shifts, no headwind, the first ship in no hurry.
- Reveal 92–103 s: Paches has just read the order when the second ship arrives.
- Honest twist 103–120 s: the ringleaders sent to Athens were executed; "more than a thousand" is a disputed figure.
- Close 120–128 s: saved by an overnight change of mind, a handful of votes, and rowers who never stopped.
